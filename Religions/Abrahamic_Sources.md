# Master Scope of this project finding lost history through the ancient history using Abrahamic religions and all source of the "Abrahamic Religions"

# Structured Breakdown of the Textual Ecosystem

A structured breakdown organizing the massive textual ecosystem into **27 distinct categories**, balancing clarity with the depth of the provided sources.

---

# Ancient Near Eastern & Proto-Abrahamic Roots

1. **Sumerian & Akkadian Religious Literature**
   Includes early creation narratives, divine myth cycles, and heroic epics such as the *Eridu Genesis*, *Enuma Elish*, the *Atrahasis Epic*, and the *Epic of Gilgamesh*.

2. **Assyrian, Hittite & Hurrian Mythologies**
   Covers royal theology, divination texts, and dragon/creation myths like the *Kumarbi Cycle*, *Song of Ullikummi*, and *Illuyanka Myth*.

3. **Canaanite, Ugaritic & Egyptian Traditions**
   Encompasses the *Baal Cycle*, *Kirta Epic*, and Egyptian underworld literature like the *Book of the Dead*.

4. **Proto-Israelite & Ancient Levant Inscriptions**
   Archaeological evidence and early Yahweh traditions, including the *Mesha Stele*, *Tel Dan Stele*, and Phoenician/Aramaic texts.

5. **Zoroastrian & Pre-Islamic Arabian Influences**
   Includes foundational texts like the *Avesta*, *Yasna*, and later Zoroastrian literature, as well as Nabataean inscriptions and Pre-Islamic poetry.

---

# Hebrew Bible & Second Temple Judaism

6. **Hebrew Bible (Tanakh) Canon**
   The traditional Masoretic texts divided into the Torah (Pentateuch), Nevi’im (Prophets), and Ketuvim (Writings).

7. **Alternate Biblical Textual Traditions**
   Variations of the biblical canon including the *Samaritan Pentateuch*, the Greek *Septuagint*, the Syriac *Peshitta*, and the Latin *Vulgate*.

8. **Jewish Apocrypha & Deuterocanonical Books**
   Historical, wisdom, and narrative literature not in the standard Hebrew canon, such as *Maccabees* (1–4), *Tobit*, *Judith*, and the *Wisdom of Solomon*.

9. **Dead Sea Scroll Library**
   Includes sectarian texts (*Community Rule*, *War Scroll*), biblical scrolls (*Great Isaiah Scroll*), and apocalyptic/calendrical texts found at Qumran.

10. **Jewish Pseudepigrapha**
    Attributed ancient texts spanning Enochic literature (1–3 Enoch), Adam and Eve traditions, Patriarchal Testaments (e.g., *Testament of the Twelve Patriarchs*), and apocalypses.

11. **Second Temple Historical & Philosophical Works**
    Includes the writings of Philo of Alexandria, Flavius Josephus (*Antiquities of the Jews*), and the *Book of Jubilees*.

---

# Rabbinic Judaism

12. **Core Rabbinic Law (Mishnah & Tosefta)**
    The foundational oral laws divided into six orders: Zeraim (Agriculture), Moed (Festivals), Nashim (Family), Nezikin (Civil/Criminal), Kodashim (Sacrifices), and Tohorot (Purity).

13. **The Talmuds**
    The comprehensive commentaries on the Mishnah, specifically the *Jerusalem Talmud* and the larger *Babylonian Talmud*.

14. **Midrashic Literature & Rabbinic Commentaries**
    Interpretive texts spanning Halakhic (legal) and Aggadic (narrative) midrashim like *Genesis Rabbah*, alongside medieval commentaries by Rashi, Ibn Ezra, and Maimonides.

---

# Early Christianity & Patristics

15. **Christian Canonical Texts (New Testament)**
    The four Gospels, Acts of the Apostles, Pauline and General Epistles, and the Book of Revelation.

16. **Apostolic Fathers & Early Liturgy**
    The earliest post-New Testament writings, including the *Didache*, *1 & 2 Clement*, Epistles of Ignatius, and early creeds (Nicene, Apostles').

17. **Patristic Literature (Early Church Fathers)**
    Theological, historical, and homiletic writings from Greek and Latin figures like Origen, Augustine, Tertullian, and Irenaeus.

---

# Christian Apocrypha, Gnosticism & Alternative Traditions

18. **Christian Apocrypha & Lost Gospels**
    Non-canonical narratives including dialogue gospels, infancy gospels (*Protoevangelium of James*), Apocryphal Acts (*Acts of Peter / Paul*), and Apocryphal Apocalypses.

19. **Nag Hammadi Library & Gnostic Scriptures**
    A massive collection of 52 esoteric texts across 13 codices, including the *Gospel of Thomas*, *Gospel of Truth*, *Apocryphon of John*, and broader Sethian / Valentinian literature.

20. **Christian Heresies & Suppressed Movements**
    Texts and traditions from marginalized early groups such as the Ebionites, Marcionites, Arians, Montanists, Adoptionists, Nestorians, and Monophysites.

21. **Regional Christian Traditions**
    Literature unique to specific ancient branches, including Syriac (*Odes of Solomon*), Ethiopian (*Kebra Nagast*), and Armenian traditions.

---

# Islamic Foundational Texts & Scholarship

22. **Qur’anic Canon & Early Traditions**
    The 114 Canonical Surahs, early manuscript evidence (e.g., Sana’a, Birmingham), and canonical variant reading traditions (Qira’at).

23. **Hadith Collections & Early Islamic History**
    The major sayings of the Prophet, divided into the Sunni *Kutub al-Sittah* (including Bukhari and Muslim) and Shia collections (like *Al-Kafi*), as well as early historical and biographical literature.

24. **Islamic Jurisprudence (Fiqh) & Tafsir**
    Qur'anic commentary (Tafsir al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir) and the major legal schools (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi’i, Hanbali, and Ja'fari).

25. **Islamic Theology (Kalam) & Philosophy**
    Theological debates (Ash’ari, Maturidi, Mu’tazilite) and major philosophical works by Al-Farabi, Avicenna, and Averroes.

---

# Mysticism, Esoterica & Visionary Literature

26. **Jewish Mysticism & Kabbalah**
    Ranges from early Hekhalot (Heavenly Palace) and Merkavah (Chariot) mysticism to core Kabbalistic texts like the *Zohar*, Lurianic Kabbalah, and later Hasidic mystical literature.

27. **Islamic Mysticism (Sufism) & Esoterica**
    Sufi poetry and metaphysics (e.g., Rumi's *Masnavi*, works of Ibn Arabi), Sufi Order teachings, and Ismaili/Druze esoteric texts.

28. **Christian Mysticism & Visionary Literature**
    Texts ranging from the asceticism of the Desert Fathers and Eastern Orthodox works (*Philokalia*) to medieval Western, Catholic, and female mystics (Hildegard of Bingen, Julian of Norwich), as well as sacred poetry like Dante's *Divine Comedy*.
